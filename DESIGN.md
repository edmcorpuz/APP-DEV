# Sugilanon design direction

Brief from the owner: an Art Deco style and feel, Filipino, and true to Sugilanon being Bisaya.
Philosophy written with the canvas-design skill. Type pairing from the ui-ux-pro-max
typography search ("Art Deco": Poiret One + Didact Gothic), extended with Josefin Sans so
headlines stay readable. Colors chosen for the brief and checked against WCAG AA.

Dial: ENERGY 2 / RHYTHM 2 / MOTION 3 for the living mural, 2 everywhere else

## Movement: Adlaw Deco

Adlaw Deco is the geometry of a sunrise drawn by a draughtsman who grew up beside the sea.
Light does not glow here; it radiates in ruled lines, fanning from a single point the way a
1930s theatre facade fans its plaster rays above the door. Every ray is counted and spaced by
hand. The composition is symmetrical and calm, built on a centre line, and its confidence comes
from that order: meticulously measured, the product of someone who has drawn ornament for a
lifetime.

Material is lacquer and capiz. Deep sea green stands in for the polished dark wood and the
water at dusk; warm ivory is the translucent shell of a capiz window with daylight behind it;
gold is the thin inlay that outlines everything precious. Gold is never a flood. It is a hairline,
a double rule, a single filled panel at the one place that matters, applied with painstaking
restraint so it still reads as gold.

Ornament has a job. A double gold frame says "this is the feature". A stepped band of waves says
"a new section begins", as a tide line marks the shore. An arched window holds the one picture on
the page. Nothing is added for its own sake; each motif repeats in the same proportions everywhere
it appears, so the whole site feels drawn from one master sheet, labored over until no line is
out of place.

Type speaks in two registers. Display letters are tall, thin and geometric, the lettering of
cinema marquees and shopfronts, used large and sparingly. Everything people actually read is set
in a plain geometric sans that never strains the eye. Small labels are spaced generously and
carry the Bisaya words of the place, quietly, beside the English, so the language is felt as
part of the architecture rather than announced.

Motion is ceremonial and brief: rays fan open once, the title settles, rows arrive in order like
guests taking their seats. Then everything is still. Anyone who asks for less motion receives the
finished, fully composed page at once. The craft is in the timing, tuned until it feels
inevitable.

## Murals

Generated by `frontend/art/murals.mjs` as flat Deco silhouettes in faceted bronze, the way
1930s Deco reliefs simplify the figure. Neither man has a surviving portrait, so both are
original stylizations and copy no statue or painting.

- **Lapu-Lapu of Mactan** (Visayas, 1521). Long hair, red putong (headcloth), Visayan pintados
  (tattoos) drawn as chevrons and limb bands, red bahag, kampilan held point-down. He stands on a
  stepped plinth at the shore under an eight-ray sun halo, flanked by two Mindanao vintas with
  striped sails. Home page masthead.
- **Sultan Kudarat of Maguindanao** (Mindanao, 1600s). Turban with a gold crest, beard, long robe
  with a red sash, a kris held point-down, under a torogan roof whose panolong beams end in
  okir-style spirals. Login and sign-up pages.
- Both stand above Deco waves and a T'nalak-inspired diamond band (T'boli weaving of Mindanao).
- The vinta stripes (red, yellow, green, blue) appear only inside the art; the interface palette
  stays sea, ivory and gold.

- **The Battle of Mactan** (panorama, story page). Lapu-Lapu raises his kampilan on the shore
  with four warriors (spears, kalasag shields) while Magellan's soldiers in morion helmets wade
  back to their longboat and two ships with red saltire sails wait beyond the reef.

### Motion (MOTION dial 3 for the mural, 2 elsewhere; every piece has a job)

The home arch and the story banner show one living scene (`src/art/world.svg`, placed by
`MuralStage.jsx`). It never reloads between the two pages, so opening the story is one
continuous movement rather than a fade.

- **Pointing at the home mural (or keyboard focus):** the layers lean with the pointer by depth
  (parallax), the rays turn, the sea drifts, the vintas bob, the palms sway, his halo pulses, his
  headcloth flutters and Lapu-Lapu raises his kampilan: the clasped blade lets go first
  (anticipation), then the arm swings up with a small overshoot (follow-through). It stops when
  you leave, so nothing on the home page moves on its own.
- **Opening the mural:** the frame grows from the arch into the banner while the camera pulls back
  from Lapu-Lapu to the whole shore of Mactan. On the way the sun rises, the Spanish ships sail
  in, the vintas drift out to sea, Magellan's soldiers fall back to their boats, the warriors rise
  one by one and his sword goes up as the camera settles. Going back reverses all of it.
- **Idle life in the banner:** the sea, boats, palms, warriors (shifting their weight) and
  Lapu-Lapu (breathing, headcloth in the wind) keep moving, with a Pause motion button.
- **Music:** "Gubat sa Baybayon", an original war-drum piece generated live in the browser
  (agung and kulintang gongs, dabakan drum, babendil timekeeper, after Mindanao gong ensembles).
  It starts only when you open the mural (browsers allow sound only after a click), fades out when
  you leave the story, and has a Play/Mute button that remembers your choice.
- With `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, nothing animates: the scene jumps straight to each state
  and the pause button is hidden as there is nothing to pause.

## Texture

A faint abaca/capiz paper grain and a T'nalak diamond lattice cover the page, and a sunburst
fans up behind the masthead. They give the flat ivory surface the life of handmade paper and tie
the whole page to Filipino weaving. Their strength is capped (lattice 10%, rays 6%, grain 7%) and
every text color was checked against the darkest texture pixel.

## Bisaya in the interface

English stays the primary text so nobody depends on a translation. Bisaya (Cebuano) phrases sit
beside it as labels, marked `lang="ceb"` so screen readers pronounce them correctly:
Bag-o (new), Uban pang mga sugilanon (more stories), Mga sugilanon gikan sa among mga magsusulat
(stories from our writers), Maayong pagbalik (welcome back), Apil na (join now), Balik (back),
Salamat sa pagbasa (thank you for reading). A native speaker should proofread these.

## Why each decision (R-31)

- Sea green, ivory, gold: lacquer, capiz and inlay; one accent (gold) carries the luxury.
- Sunburst with eight main rays: the Deco fan, and a quiet nod to the eight-rayed sun of the Philippine flag.
- Wave bands as section dividers: the Visayan sea, drawn as a Deco stepped pattern; they mark section starts.
- Arched window for the artwork: the arch of Filipino church windows and Deco facades.
- Symmetric, centred masthead: Deco facades are symmetric; the story list and article stay left-aligned for reading.

## Round 5: poster surfaces and Mindanao motifs (22 Nov 2026)

References: 1930s Deco travel and film posters (banded dusk skies, rays fanning from the horizon,
metallic display lettering). Asked for: gradient textures, felt paper, Art Deco, Mindanaoan.
Layout, copy and components are unchanged; only surfaces changed (all in `frontend/src/index.css`).

- **Felt paper** (`--felt`): fine fibres mottled by a slow noise cloud, on the page, header, cards, inputs and buttons.
- **Poster sky** (`--sky`, `--glow`, `--rays`): the masthead is full-bleed, with a dusk gradient, a sunrise glow behind the mural and stronger rays. Full bleed uses `cqw` on a body container so it never adds a horizontal scrollbar.
- **Metallic lettering** (`--metal`): gold gradient on the masthead, form and editor titles and the footer line, one gradient per line (`1lh`). Falls back to plain text in forced-colors mode.
- **Okir** (Maranao vine scroll, `--okir`): replaces the double rule beside centred labels.
- **Vinta stripes** (Zamboanga sail, `--vinta`): a 4px band across the top of the header.
- **T'nalak lattice** (T'boli, South Cotabato): kept from round 3.
- Gradients on the lead card, story rows (hover), role choices, the primary button and the gold tags.
- Contrast re-checked: gold text darkened to `#5F4614` to keep 4.5:1 over sky, rays and felt together.
- **The mural** (`frontend/art/murals.mjs`, world scene): SVG gradients for a banded poster sky down to an amber horizon, hazier far hills (aerial depth), rays that fade from pale gold at the sun to almost nothing at the edges, a glowing sun core, and sunlight on the water that rises with the sun and shimmers (`.w-glint`). Felt grain and a vignette sit over the murals as a CSS layer (cheap during animation). The PNG murals keep flat fills, with the same felt overlay.
- **Music** (`frontend/src/audio/warDrums.js`): "Bangon, Mactan" (rise, Mactan), an original battle anthem at 80 BPM. Style borrowed from modern epic-rock anthems (half-time stomp, booming kicks, huge claps, a dark minor choir on Dm-Bb-F-C, a build over 16 bars); no melody is taken from any song. Mindanao voices carry it: agung on the downbeats, a kulintang ostinato and hook on a D minor pentatonic row, dabakan rolls into each section, babendil off-beats. Checked by level meter (builds from 0.10 to 0.17 RMS, peak 0.77, no clipping), not by ear.
- **Figures** (warriors, Lapu-Lapu, soldiers, ships, vintas, palms): each shape is lit warm from above and falls into shadow below (one gradient per colour), and every corner is rounded with a small curve. Every figure has its own stance and motion, each arm swinging about its own shoulder: Lapu-Lapu stays upright at the centre, kampilan raised; one warrior winds up and hurls (the spear stays in hand), one hops behind his shield and parries low, one swings his spear overhead, one levels his spear and lunges; on the Spanish side one soldier stumbles on the reef, one backs toward the boat, one ducks a thrown spear. No one is struck or hurt.
